# PDPA launch checks for Manhunt IX

Internal release checklist for the organiser who operates this portal. This records matters the code and current public pages cannot establish; it is not a certification of compliance or legal advice. Do not publish policy statements about unverified practices as facts.

## Before publishing the privacy policy and terms

- [ ] **Identify the organisation responsible.** Record its legal/operating name, Singapore contact address (if applicable), and who controls the portal data. Confirm who can make decisions about collection, use, disclosure, and retention. The repository identifies no operator or legal entity.
- [ ] **Confirm DPO designation and mailbox coverage.** The privacy policy lists the supplied `xyz2011a@gmail.com` address for the Data Protection Officer. Confirm who is formally designated to implement privacy practices and handle requests, and who monitors this mailbox if they are away. The terms list `noreply.manhunt@gmail.com` for general event queries only.
- [ ] **Confirm registration phone purpose and necessity.** New player registration requires a phone number, but code does not verify ownership and no SMS is sent. Organisers must document the specific account-verification purpose, why the number is necessary, who can access it, whether a less intrusive method would work, and when it will be removed. Do not describe it as phone verification or SMS authentication.
- [ ] **Set the event’s age and guardian process before collecting children’s data.** Under-13s may participate or appear in evidence, or their ages may be unknown. The portal gives a contact route but does not verify ages or guardian consent. Decide whether under-13 participation will be allowed. Use a proportionate age check that collects the minimum information needed. Before collecting an under-13’s account details or accepting evidence that identifies them, explain the purposes and visibility to the parent or legal guardian, verify their authority, record their consent, and explain how to withdraw it. If the process is unavailable, keep under-13s out of registration and identifiable evidence until it is in place. Ages 13–17 may consent where the notice and consequences are readily understandable; seek guardian consent if the child may not understand. The broader guidance treats minors as under 21 and notes legal capacity can depend on other law.
- [ ] **Minimise evidence about other people.** Tell players to avoid uploading bystanders or unrelated personal information, and obtain permission where appropriate. State who reviews evidence and where it may be shown (the code exposes evidence to the uploader’s group and authorised admins). Establish a way to flag/remove a third party’s image, voice, or identifying details. Explain that image metadata can include GPS, capture time, and device model and is available to admins on demand; invite users to remove location metadata or avoid uploading sensitive locations where it is not needed. Review the camera-capture report (picker open/return and file time) as well as EXIF metadata.
- [ ] **Set and apply a retention schedule.** Code sets incomplete upload expiry at 24 hours with scheduled cleanup, but there is no general deletion schedule for accounts, submissions, originals/previews, event history, notifications, delivery records, or backups. Account removal clears account details but leaves a record of the deletion and can leave event history and evidence that still identify the person. Group deletion can also leave event records. Decide the purpose and end date for each category, who deletes or de-identifies records, how originals and previews are removed from R2, and when backups and logs expire. Confirm what Cloudflare, Google, Resend (if used), and push providers retain. Apply the schedule and align the policy with it.
- [ ] **Verify overseas transfer safeguards.** Production bindings use Cloudflare D1/R2 and Worker hosting; Google supplies sign-in and may send Gmail; email delivery can instead use Resend; browser/device push services deliver notifications. Confirm the actual production providers, countries/regions, subprocessors, contractual terms, security, and deletion/retention settings. Keep records showing the transfer safeguards and that the overseas recipient is bound to a standard of protection comparable to the PDPA. The repository does not establish actual deployment regions or provider terms.
- [ ] **Confirm how withdrawal and account closure work.** Publish a contact route for consent withdrawal and privacy complaints. Explain likely consequences such as losing sign-in, group participation, or notifications, and how organisers will stop the relevant collection/use/disclosure while preserving any specific records that must be kept for legitimate event, security, or legal purposes. The account has no self-service deletion path; admins have a limited player/group deletion feature. Do not imply users can delete their own account in Settings.

## Handle requests and complaints

1. Route privacy requests from the published channel to the DPO/authorised organiser. Verify identity proportionately, log the date, request, scope, owner, response, and any reason for refusal or delay; keep the request itself access-controlled.
2. For an **access request**, locate the account’s registration/contact details, Google identity, phone, group and event records, evidence/metadata, notifications, and relevant use/disclosure records. Provide the personal data and information about how it was used or disclosed in the past year, subject to applicable PDPA exceptions and third-party privacy protections.
3. For a **correction request**, check the source and update inaccurate/incomplete personal data, and correct downstream records where required. Tell the requester if correction cannot be made promptly.
4. For **withdrawal**, clarify the purposes and channels concerned, explain likely consequences, and stop collection/use/disclosure for the withdrawn purpose when the withdrawal takes effect after reasonable notice. Make sure agents and providers handling data for us stop too, unless continued processing is allowed or required by law. Record any lead time and any permitted continued retention or use and its basis. Withdrawal does not automatically erase every event record.
5. Respond as soon as reasonably possible. If unable to provide access or make a correction within 30 days, inform the individual in writing within those 30 days of when the response will be made. Escalate a complaint to the DPO, investigate it, document the outcome, and explain the available PDPC complaint route if unresolved.

## Security incident response

- [ ] Give organisers and hosting/provider contacts one monitored route to report suspected loss, unauthorised access, disclosure, alteration, or destruction of account data or evidence. Escalate immediately to the DPO and preserve relevant logs and evidence.
- [ ] Contain access, secure affected accounts/credentials, identify the data and people affected, and assess harm and scale promptly. Record the timeline, decisions, containment, remediation, and any provider coordination.
- [ ] If the organisation determines the breach is notifiable, notify the PDPC as soon as practicable and no later than three calendar days after that determination. Notify affected individuals as soon as practicable where required, at the same time as or after notifying the PDPC. Use the PDPC breach reporting service and current guidance to assess the threshold and notification content.
- [ ] Review lessons, access controls, vendor configuration, and whether notices or procedures need updating.
